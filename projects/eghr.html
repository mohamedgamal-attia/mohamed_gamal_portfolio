EJAD EGHREnterprise Employee Portal

A portal-first HR experience built on Odoo 18.

Employees get one branded place to check in, raise any HR request, follow its approval, and read what HR published — in Arabic or English, light or dark, on a phone or a desktop. The Odoo back office stays where it belongs: behind the portal.

01 · The problem

HR software people actually open

The HR data already lived in Odoo. The problem was the front door: asking every employee to learn an ERP back office to book three days off is how a rollout dies.

01

Portal-first, back office hidden. Employees never see an Odoo menu, a technical model name or an internal view. They see one branded portal.

02

One coherent set of HR services. Time off, advances, loans, tickets, visas, trips, custody and the rest had to feel like one product, not eleven forms.

03

Approvals employees can follow. A request is only useful if the person who raised it can see where it is and who has it.

04

Attendance that cannot be faked. Check-in is tied to the office location and validated on the server, not in the browser.

05

Arabic and English, phone and desktop. Both languages are first-class, and the same journeys work at 360 px and 1920 px.

06

Extensible without a rewrite. HR adds a service; the portal picks it up.

03 · Design system

The rules the portal is built from

Eleven services, two languages and two themes only stay coherent if the surface decisions are made once. These are the ones that mattered.

Surface & elevation

Four surface levels — canvas, panel, raised panel, divider — so a card never invents its own background.

Status semantics

Approved, pending, rejected, in review. One colour per meaning, applied the same way on a pill, a timeline node and a row.

05 · Request centre

One inbox for raising, one for deciding

Every service writes into the same request spine, so an employee has exactly two places to look: My Requests for what they raised, and My Actions for what is waiting on them. What appears in either is decided by record rules, not by the menu.

My Requests

Every request the employee raised, across all eleven services, with its current stage and the service it came from.

My Actions

Requests waiting on this person because of their role in the approval route — line manager, HR, or a delegate.

Request detail

The submitted values, the attachment, and an approval timeline that names each step and who currently holds it.

06 · Attendance

Check-in is a server decision

The browser supplies coordinates; it does not supply the verdict. The portal sends the position with the check-in call and the server compares it against the configured office geofence before any attendance record is written. A spoofed client gets a rejection, not a record.

Scroll for all seven states

·

Geofence held in configuration. Office coordinates and radius are data, so a new branch needs a record rather than a release.

·

Every state is designed. Permission refused, position unavailable and out-of-range all have a screen — they are not left to a browser alert.

·

Worked hours are computed. Check-out reads the stored check-in rather than trusting a second client timestamp.

·

History stays visible. The employee can see their own month without asking HR for a report.

08 · Announcements

Published once, targeted per employee

HR writes an announcement in both languages and chooses who should see it — everyone, a department, or a branch. Resolution happens per employee, so the dashboard notice, the list and the detail page all show the same filtered set.

How a request travels

Nothing reaches the employee without passing the controller and security layer, and nothing new is added to the portal without being registered as a service first.

  3. Security boundary

    Portal controllers + security

12 · Engineering highlights

The decisions worth defending

01

Dynamic employee-service registry

Services are configuration, not code branches. The catalogue, dashboard shortcuts and request spine all read the same registry.

02

Unified My Requests / My Actions

One request spine behind eleven services, split into two views by the employee's role in the approval route.

03

Server-side attendance geofencing

The client sends coordinates; the server decides. Out-of-range check-ins are rejected before a record exists.

04

Arabic RTL and English LTR

Full mirroring from one template set — layout, rails, icons, tables and numerals.

05

Light and dark themes

Two token sets over one component layer, so new components inherit both.

06

Targeted bilingual announcements

One record carries both languages and its audience; resolution happens per employee.

07

Permission-aware portal

Access rights and record rules decide what each route returns — not the navigation.

08

Responsive employee journeys

Every journey — check in, raise, approve, read — completes on a phone as well as a desktop.

09

Odoo-core-safe customisation

Custom modules and inheritance only, so the work survives the next Odoo version.
